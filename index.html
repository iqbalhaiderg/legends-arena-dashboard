5AI Budget Planning

Where to point cash over the next 2–3 quarters
1

Clear vendor debt before touching payouts

A meaningful share of expenses is still vendor-debt repayment for original construction (Bhabi, Samrat, Baharul). Retiring it early is the single biggest lever on both cash flow and reported net margin.

Priority: high
2

Set a monthly reserve before distributing

Loss months happen from lumpy debt and maintenance payments. Hold back roughly one month of average opex as a buffer before any partner distribution, on top of the 15% reserve fund.

Priority: high
3

Right-size staff cost against off-peak hours

Staff salaries run at a fixed monthly cost against a court that's occupied a small fraction of the time. Rather than cutting headcount, redirect idle daytime hours toward the initiatives below — the fixed cost is already being paid for, so filling it is close to pure margin.

Priority: medium
4

Budget a small marketing line from a debt-free month's surplus

In months with zero vendor-debt drag, earmark 5–8% of net profit toward local PR/social push aimed at filling morning and midday slots (see insight 7).

Priority: medium

6Other AI Suggestions

Growth, pricing and risk observations from the booking data
5

The evening window is the real constraint

The evening window drives most of revenue from a small slice of daily hours. That window is very likely at or near physical capacity most weekdays. Growth from here comes less from "more marketing" for peak hours and more from shifting demand into adjacent hours, or adding a second turf/court if land allows.

Growth
6

Client concentration risk

The top client accounts for a large share of YTD revenue. Worth understanding whether this is a reseller/team organizer; a preferential block-booking rate could lock in the relationship, but losing this single account would be a visible dent in monthly income.

Risk
7

Off-peak hours are essentially unused

Morning and midday hours see very little demand. Consider a steep off-peak discount for school/corporate five-a-side leagues, coaching camps, or women-only sessions to convert dead hours into incremental revenue with near-zero marginal cost.

Growth
8

Discounting eats into margin

A meaningful share of gross billing is discounted YTD. Peak-hour slots are already demand-constrained, so discounting there mostly gives away margin; consider reserving discounts for off-peak fill-rate campaigns instead of ad-hoc peak-hour negotiation.

Pricing
9

Cash-heavy collection is a control risk

Most recorded payments are cash, with little use of bKash/Nagad/bank transfer despite those methods being available. Moving more bookings to mobile/bank payment would cut cash-handling risk and give partners an independent reconciliation trail.

Risk
10

Watch for one-off maintenance spikes

Maintenance spend is lumpy month to month. If this is wear-related, a small monthly maintenance reserve would smooth cash flow better than absorbing repairs as one-off shocks.
